Normalization model of attention · eccentricity × spatial frequency

Does feature-based attention select the stimulus or the cortex?

Attend a grating at one eccentricity and measure the population contrast response to an ignored probe somewhere else. Both models spread gain across the whole field, but over different swaths of the neural image, so they predict contrast gain at different probe spatial frequencies.

R(x, f)= A(x, f) · E(x, f)nS(x, f) + σn

Only the attention field A differs between the two models. Stimulus drive E, suppressive pool S and the readout are shared.

What the simulation assumes

Neural image. Units are laid out by cortical distance along one meridian and by preferred spatial frequency. Receptive fields (1 mm sd) and the suppressive pool have a fixed size in millimetres of cortex, so both grow with eccentricity in degrees. At each eccentricity, preferred frequencies are spread around the local peak p(e).

Attention fields. Both are spatially global and pass through the attended grating. Stimulus-based puts the gain ridge at the attended cpd at every eccentricity. Cortical-based rescales it, either with cortical magnification (constant cycles per millimetre) or with the local preferred frequency.

Measurement. The voxel is a weighted sum of the units at the probe’s cortical location. Its weights are a band in SF centred on the local preferred SF. The width of that band is solved at each eccentricity so that the model voxel’s SF tuning has the measured bandwidth, by default 3 octaves at the fovea rising to 4.3 at 9° after Aghajari, Vinke & Ling (2020). That makes the band narrower than the population feeding the suppressive pool, so Rmax can move a little with attention as well as c50. Contrast response is sampled at nine contrasts from 1 to 100% and fitted with a Naka–Rushton function by least squares; c50, Rmax and n are those estimates.

Defaults. Normalization follows Reynolds & Heeger (2009). M(e) = 17.3 / (e + 0.75) mm/deg after Horton & Hoyt (1991). Preferred period 0.12e + 0.35 deg after Broderick, Simoncelli & Winawer (2022). σ is scaled so a full-field grating at the preferred frequency has an unattended c50 of σ.
